What a rule looks like

a claim is payable
    if the claim is for a damage
    and the damage is reported within the time limit
    and the damage is accidental.

Ask which claim is payable and the answer comes with its reasons:

claim one is payable, because the burst pipe is accidental, according to the loss adjuster, as stated in report LA-17 at page 3.

Leave the adjuster's judgment out, and the answer says what is still missing instead of guessing. That is the whole program, not a summary of one: open it in the editor, run the query, and click the answer for its explanation.

For a whole policy, open Medicare's coverage of power wheelchairs and scooters (LCD L33789): a claim that is paid, criterion by criterion, each with the passage of the policy that decides it, and one that is not, with the stage it fails at and what would change the answer. The same rules, as their author sees them: the policy in the editor.

Not only English: the same rules can be written in Portuguese, Spanish, French or Italian, and the keywords, the explanations and the error messages follow the language (an example in Portuguese).

One language between your rule systems

Your rules already exist, in a policy administration suite, a rule engine or the wording itself. We read them into Logical English, with their tests, and check the result against the system they came from. For five systems we write them back out again.

  • Read from what you run today. Open the other system's file: it arrives as English rules, its rate tables as decision tables, its test cases as scenarios. What could not be translated is listed, never dropped.
  • One language, two kinds of rule. Rating, eligibility and coverage are timeless rules. Claims, payments and deadlines happen over time: those are for LPS, Logic Production Systems, which this server runs. Both are written in the same English, and both explain their answers.
  • A way back, where there is one. An export never loses meaning silently: what the target cannot say makes the export refuse, and name the lines.
