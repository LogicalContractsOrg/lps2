# Privacy notice for the hosted sites

*Kind: policy · Audience: everyone who uses the hosted sites · Status: current (2026-10-21: keys for the tools of the assistants)*

This notice tells you which personal data the two hosted sites keep, and why. It also
tells you how to make us correct or remove that data. Personal data is any
information about a person whom somebody can identify, for example an email address.

The two sites are:

- `le2.logicalcontracts.com`, the Logical English editor, and
- `lps2.logicalcontracts.com`, the Logic Production System (LPS) editor.

## Who is responsible

**Renting Point - Serviços de Informática, Lda.** operates the sites. This company is
registered in Portugal. It operates the `logicalcontracts.com` domain. The General
Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the data protection law of the European Union.
In the words of the GDPR, the company is the *controller*. The controller is the
party that decides which personal data is kept and why.

For each question or request about your data, write to
**support@logicalcontracts.com**.

## The short version

- You can use the two sites without an account. You do not have to tell us who
  you are.
- If you sign in, we receive your email address. We receive nothing else about
  you.
- We keep your email address only in two cases: you hold a licence, or we made a
  password account for you.
- We do not keep the programs that you write together with your name or your email
  address.
- There is no advertising and there are no tracking cookies. We do not sell or
  share data.

## Signing in

You can sign in with one of these: a Google account, a GitHub account, or an email
address and password that we made for you.

When you sign in with Google or GitHub, these things occur:

1. The site sends you to Google or GitHub.
2. You sign in there.
3. Google or GitHub makes sure that the email address is really your address.
4. Google or GitHub tells us one thing: your email address.

We do not ask for your name, your photograph, your contacts or any other thing.

After you sign in, your browser keeps a small file with the name `lc_session`. This
type of file is a cookie. The cookie contains your email address and the method that
you used to sign in. With the cookie, the two sites recognise you. The cookie expires
after 14 days, or when you sign out. While a sign-in with Google or GitHub continues,
there is a second cookie, `lc_oauth`. This cookie lives for a maximum of ten
minutes. It makes sure that the answer from Google or GitHub comes back to the
browser that asked for it.

Some persons sign in with Google or GitHub and do not hold a licence. The sites
themselves keep nothing about these persons. The servers record that a sign-in
occurred, and the method of the sign-in. The servers do not record who signed in.

## What we keep, and why

| What | Why | How long |
|---|---|---|
| For a licence holder: the email address, the licence, the expiry date of the licence, and a reference to the invoice or agreement | To give the licence holder what the licence pays for | Until some months after the licence ends, or until you ask us to remove it |
| For a password account: the email address and a scrambled form of the password (a *hash*; nobody can read the password back from the hash) | To let that person sign in | Until the account is closed, or until you ask us to remove it |

These two lists are the only places where we keep email addresses. The legal basis
to keep them is the licence agreement, or your own request for an account (GDPR
Article 6(1)(b)).

## Your programs

To answer a query, the server receives the program that you work on. The server
calculates the answer and sends the answer back. The server keeps the program in its
memory only while you use the program. The server does not keep the program with
your account or your email address. Some features write a working copy of the
program to the temporary space of the server. Examples of these features are the
assistants and the importers of other systems. The server clears that space when the
server starts again.

## The assistants

Some features of the editors use a large language model. A large language model is a
program from a different company that writes text. These features help you to write
Logical English. When you use one of these features, we send your text to the
company of the model that you selected (for example OpenAI, Anthropic or Google).
We send the text that you give to the assistant. We also send your program when the
feature needs it. That company uses the text under its own terms. Do not give
personal data to the assistants if you do not want that company to receive it.

## Error reports and visit counts

When an error occurs on a site, the site sends a report of the error to **Sentry**.
Sentry is a service that collects error reports. The report contains the name of the
operation that failed and the error message. The report does not contain your
program, your query or your email address. The *Feedback* form also sends your
message to Sentry. It sends a name and an email address only if you type them.

The sites count visits with **Cloudflare Web Analytics**. Cloudflare Web Analytics
records which pages people visit, from which country, and with which type of browser
and device. Cloudflare Web Analytics does not set a cookie. It does not follow you
from one site to a different site.

## Keys for the tools of the assistants

The two sites also answer programs that use the Model Context Protocol (MCP). MCP
is a common method for an assistant, such as Claude Code, to use the tools of a
server. You can use these tools without a key. A key gives a larger number of
calls.

- A key is a long text that starts with `lc_`. We make a key for a person, or for
  an event, for example a lecture. The key contains the email address of the person
  or the name of the event, and the last day on which the key is valid. The sites do
  not keep the keys. A key that we withdraw goes on a list. The list contains only
  the identifier of the key: twelve letters and digits.
- To give each client a fair share, the servers count the calls of each client: for
  each key, or for each internet address if there is no key. The LPS server also
  counts the seconds of the programs that each client runs. The servers count in
  the same way the requests of the assistants of the editors that use a key of the
  server. They do not count a request that uses your own key. The servers keep these
  counts in memory, for the current day only. The total of an event key is kept
  until the event ends.
- The servers do not keep the facts and the questions that a call sends. These can
  contain personal data about a real case.

## Cookies

The sites use only the cookies that are necessary for what you asked for:

- `lc_session` and `lc_oauth`, for the sign-in, as the section above describes;
- on the Logical English site, `le_ui_lang`, which keeps the language that you
  selected for the menus.

The sites do not use these cookies for advertising or for tracking. Thus the sites
do not ask for your consent before they set them.

## Other companies that handle data for us

- **Fly.io** runs the two servers, in London (United Kingdom). The European
  Union recognises that the United Kingdom protects personal data adequately.
- **Google** and **GitHub**, only when you select to sign in with them.
- **Sentry** and **Cloudflare**, for error reports and visit counts.
- The company of the language model that you select, when you use an assistant.

Some of these companies can possibly handle data outside the European Union, which
includes the United States. Each company does this under its standard data
protection terms. These terms include the safeguards that the GDPR requires for such
transfers.

## Your rights

At any time, you can write to us at **support@logicalcontracts.com** and ask us:

- for a copy of the data that we keep about you;
- to correct the data;
- to remove the data. For a licence holder, when we remove the email address, the
  licence ends;
- to stop the use of the data.

We answer in a maximum of one month. If you are not satisfied with our answer, you
can make a complaint to the Portuguese data protection authority, the *Comissão
Nacional de Proteção de Dados* (CNPD), at [www.cnpd.pt](https://www.cnpd.pt). You
can also make a complaint to the authority of the country where you live.
